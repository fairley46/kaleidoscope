# Strategy Lens

## Lens Name
Strategy Lens

## Purpose
Look at the problem as a strategy question: direction, positioning, bet size, and whether the framing matches the strategy the organization claims to be pursuing.

## When to Use
- When the change is justified as strategic leverage or platform direction.
- When options differ mainly in which future they optimize for.
- When strategy language is present but the underlying bet is still fuzzy.

## Key Questions
- What strategic bet is this problem really about?
- Which future does each option make more likely?
- What would we have to stop doing if this direction is real?
- How does this reinforce or contradict the current strategy narrative?
- What evidence would falsify the strategic premise?

## What This Lens Looks For
- Clarity of the strategic premise versus slogans.
- Alignment between the problem framing and stated strategy.
- Opportunity cost of committing attention here.
- Whether the bet is reversible or path-defining.
- Gaps between strategy decks and operating reality.

## Common Risks / Failure Modes
- Calling something strategic to skip ordinary scrutiny.
- Pursuing a direction that strategy no longer supports.
- Spreading attention across bets that fight each other.
- Treating a tactic as a strategy.
- Leaving the kill criteria undefined.

## Signals or Evidence to Consider
- Current strategy narrative and recent strategy changes.
- Portfolio of related bets and their sequencing.
- Customer or market signals that motivate the direction.
- Prior strategic initiatives and what they taught.
- Explicit stop/continue criteria if any exist.

## Output Expectations
- Separate the strategic premise from the delivery plan.
- Surface conflicts with roadmap and capacity views.
- Prefer open questions about the bet and its falsifiers.
- Keep optional next steps secondary.

## Recommendation Style
Skeptical of slogan-level strategy. Name the bet and the tradeoff.
