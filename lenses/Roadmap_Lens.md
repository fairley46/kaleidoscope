# Roadmap Lens

## Lens Name
Roadmap Lens

## Purpose
Look at the problem through sequencing and commitment: what is already on the roadmap, what would move, and whether the timing implied by the framing is real.

## When to Use
- When the change competes with existing roadmap commitments.
- When phasing, migration windows, or near-term delivery matter.
- When strategy sounds clear but the sequence is still hand-wavy.

## Key Questions
- What has to move on the roadmap for this to happen?
- What is the smallest useful slice that could land first?
- Which dependencies set the true earliest date?
- What slips if this proceeds now?
- How does the proposed sequence interact with other committed work?

## What This Lens Looks For
- Explicit sequencing versus implied urgency.
- Conflicts between strategy ambition and roadmap capacity.
- Migration and coexistence windows.
- Near-term delivery risk created by a long-horizon bet.
- Places where "now" is a preference rather than a constraint.

## Common Risks / Failure Modes
- Declaring urgency without a sequence.
- Pretending parallel work has no interaction cost.
- Parking migration risk after the first launch milestone.
- Letting strategy override roadmap reality without renaming the tradeoff.
- Building a plan that only works if nothing else moves.

## Signals or Evidence to Consider
- Current roadmap commitments and freeze windows.
- Dependency graphs and shared-team bottlenecks.
- Prior phased rollouts in the same domain.
- Release calendars and hard external dates.
- What teams say they would drop first under pressure.

## Output Expectations
- Make timing and sequencing tensions visible.
- Call out strategy-versus-roadmap conflicts when both lenses are in play.
- Prefer open questions about what moves and what waits.
- Keep optional next steps secondary.

## Recommendation Style
Concrete about sequence. Do not invent dates or capacity numbers.
