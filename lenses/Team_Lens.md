# Team Lens

## Lens Name
Team Lens

## Purpose
Look at the problem from the teams that will carry the work: capacity, ownership, skills, handoffs, and what gets harder or easier in daily delivery.

## When to Use
- When ownership, staffing, or team boundaries are part of the ambiguity.
- When a shared platform or process change will land on specific teams.
- When success depends on how teams actually work, not only on architecture.

## Key Questions
- Which team owns the outcome after the initial push fades?
- What load does this add for the teams closest to the work?
- Where do handoffs get clearer, and where do they get messier?
- What skills or practices does the team need that it may not have yet?
- How would the team explain this change to itself in one sentence?

## What This Lens Looks For
- Real ownership versus nominal ownership.
- Capacity and focus cost for the affected teams.
- Handoff friction between platform and product teams.
- Skill gaps and learning load.
- Team-level incentives that fight the proposed path.

## Common Risks / Failure Modes
- Assigning ownership without capacity.
- Underestimating the team's interrupt and support load.
- Designing a path that only works if teams behave unlike they do today.
- Concentrating knowledge in one team until it becomes a bottleneck.
- Ignoring the team's existing roadmap commitments.

## Signals or Evidence to Consider
- Team charters, staffing, and current roadmap load.
- On-call and support patterns for related systems.
- Past handoff failures between the same teams.
- Skill distribution and bus-factor risk.
- How the team has responded to similar changes.

## Output Expectations
- Make team impact concrete without inventing headcount numbers.
- Surface ownership and capacity tensions early.
- Prefer open questions about who carries the work day to day.
- Keep optional next steps secondary.

## Recommendation Style
Grounded in how teams actually operate. Say the hard ownership questions plainly.
