# Company Lens

## Lens Name
Company Lens

## Purpose
Look at the problem from the company's point of view: mandate, market position, operating constraints, and what the company is trying to become. This lens asks how the problem sits inside the larger enterprise story.

## When to Use
- When the change touches company-wide standards, shared platforms, or enterprise risk.
- When local team preferences may conflict with company direction.
- When the framing is mostly technical and the company context is still thin.

## Key Questions
- What company outcome does this problem actually touch?
- Does the current framing match how the company talks about priorities?
- What company-level constraint would reshape the options if named plainly?
- Where would this create company-wide precedent, for better or worse?
- What would leadership need to believe for this path to stay coherent?

## What This Lens Looks For
- Fit with stated company strategy and operating model.
- Enterprise risk, compliance, and reputation implications.
- Precedent effects across business units or product lines.
- Gaps between company ambition and local capacity.
- Places where company language is covering for an unresolved tradeoff.

## Common Risks / Failure Modes
- Treating a local optimization as if it were a company imperative.
- Ignoring company constraints until late in the work.
- Creating a shared capability the company cannot staff or govern.
- Using company mandate language without a concrete company outcome.
- Solving for one unit in a way that fragments the company further.

## Signals or Evidence to Consider
- Company strategy docs, OKRs, and stated operating principles.
- Cross-unit dependencies and shared-service charters.
- Prior enterprise initiatives that stuck or stalled.
- Risk, compliance, and audit expectations tied to the domain.
- How similar company-level moves were explained to the organization.

## Output Expectations
- Surface the company-level stakes without inventing metrics.
- Name where company and local views may diverge.
- Leave open questions about mandate, precedent, and enterprise fit.
- Keep any next steps secondary to the exploration.

## Recommendation Style
Plain and direct. Prefer questions and tensions over verdicts.
